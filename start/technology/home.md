## Why another Web Builder

AmadeusWeb Spring is for people who want a durable digital home for a mission, a practice, a team or a network. It is not another dashboard that hides content behind a proprietary database. It is a **file-based, HTML-first builder** in which folders, pages and supporting documents remain visible to the people creating them.

The platform is deliberately modest. It brings together Markdown, HTML, PHP, sheets, presentations, forms, media and AI conversation exports so that a website can grow as an organised body of work. A three-year outline gives the work direction while content is built iteratively.

---

## Why Devops is Moot

For this kind of publishing, much of the conventional DevOps stack solves problems that do not need to exist. There is no application database to migrate, no login system to maintain for ordinary public content and no deployment pipeline required for every small editorial change.

The practical loop is simpler: edit locally, review offline, record the change in Git and upload the approved files by FTP to Apache. This does not make engineering discipline moot. It makes the operational surface small enough for a team to understand and manage directly.

---

## Why Still PHP / Shared Linux Hosting

PHP remains a useful fit because it is mature, widely hosted and well suited to assembling pages from files, folders and structured content. Apache and shared Linux hosting keep the infrastructure affordable and familiar, while Spring provides the conventions that make the site coherent.

This approach avoids paying for a large application platform when the core need is reliable public publishing. Hosting is managed by Imran and is provided through an annual hosting fee alongside the platform fee.

## Why Git is the Way

Git gives content a history. It shows what changed, when it changed and allows earlier versions to be recovered. A public site can also make its repository public, supporting transparency and making the source of the published work inspectable.

Git works especially well here because the source is made of ordinary files. Teams can use TortoiseGit or another Git client without handing their entire publishing process to a hosted CMS. Content is backed up, reviewed and developed offline before it reaches the server.

---

## Why Technical Self Sufficiency and DIY Matters

A mission should not depend on one person knowing an opaque dashboard or on a vendor continuing to support an undocumented workflow. Training in VS Code, Git, folder organisation, content planning and Spring conventions gives teams the ability to understand and extend their own site.

DIY does not mean everyone must become a professional programmer. It means the people responsible for the work can read the structure, make informed changes, review contributions and know how publication happens. Specialists can still help, but the organisation is not helpless without them.

---

## Why Plain Text is Important (this includes sheets and presentations)

Plain text is durable, searchable, portable and easy to compare in Git. Markdown, HTML, PHP and TSV can be opened and understood with common tools, while structured sheets can feed landing pages without taking ownership away from the article files themselves.

Presentations can be authored as Reveal.js or Slides decks and remain part of the same organised content system. Photos, embedded YouTube and Spotify media, email templates and AI chat exports can sit alongside the pages they support. PDFs are retained where they are useful for legacy content or document repositories, but responsive HTML is preferred for new material.

---

## Why Granular Access and Federated Content Matters

People do not all need the same view of a mission. Spring supports a hierarchy of sections, nodes, sub nodes and pages, with navigation that mirrors the folder structure. Related sites or initiatives can remain distinct while forming a federated network.

This makes it possible to give each audience an appropriate path through the work, let different contributors own sections and keep shared assets outside individual site folders through the `cdn` variable. Public content stays transparent, while confidential organisational material can remain outside the public publishing repository.
